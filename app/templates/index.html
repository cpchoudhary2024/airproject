PM2.5 Correction Formula Comparison

Compare how three correction equations translate raw sensor readings. The main analysis uses EPA Barkjohn.

EPA Barkjohn (2021) PM2.5 = 0.524 × raw − 0.0862 × RH + 5.75 Standard for PurpleAir sensors; humidity-corrected. Used for all main analysis.
LRAPA PM2.5 = 0.5 × raw − 0.66 Lane Regional Air Protection Agency. Simple linear, no RH correction.
AQ&U PM2.5 = 0.778 × raw + 2.65 University of Utah / AQ&U network correction.

    Methods & Standards

    Every metric on this platform is traceable to a published method or an official health standard. These are the primary sources — citable directly in research, regulatory, and community documentation.

    WHO 2021 — 24-hour PM2.5 15 µg/m³ Air Quality Guideline (health-based)
    WHO 2021 — annual PM2.5 5 µg/m³ Long-term exposure guideline
    US EPA — 24-hour NAAQS 35 µg/m³ Regulatory standard
    US EPA — annual NAAQS (2024) 9.0 µg/m³ Strengthened from 12.0 in 2024
    1. Barkjohn, K.K., Gantt, B., & Clements, A.L. (2021). Development and application of a United States-wide correction for PM2.5 data collected with the PurpleAir sensor. Atmospheric Measurement Techniques, 14(6), 4617–4637. doi:10.5194/amt-14-4617-2021 Used for the default humidity-corrected PM2.5 equation.
    2. World Health Organization (2021). WHO global air quality guidelines: particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide. Geneva: WHO. who.int Source of the WHO 15 µg/m³ health threshold.
    3. U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (2024). National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) for Particulate Matter — Final Reconsideration. epa.gov Source of the EPA 35 µg/m³ (24-h) and 9.0 µg/m³ (annual) standards.
    4. Cleveland, R.B., Cleveland, W.S., McRae, J.E., & Terpenning, I. (1990). STL: A Seasonal-Trend Decomposition Procedure Based on Loess. Journal of Official Statistics, 6(1), 3–73. Basis of the trend / seasonality / residual decomposition.
    5. Lane Regional Air Protection Agency (LRAPA). PurpleAir correction factor.  ·  Sayahi, T., Butterfield, A., & Kelly, K.E. (AQ&U, Univ. of Utah). Low-cost sensor calibration. Alternative correction formulas offered in the comparison panel.
    6. U.S. EPA (2021). AirNow Fire and Smoke Map — PurpleAir sensor correction methodology. airnow.gov Reference implementation of sensor correction for public reporting.

    Air quality categories on this platform are expressed against health-based PM2.5 mass thresholds (WHO / EPA) rather than a single AQI index, to preserve the underlying concentration and its uncertainty. Where an "AQI" value is shown it is the PM2.5 sub-index only: the official U.S. AQI is the maximum of several pollutant sub-indices (PM2.5, PM10, O3, CO, SO2, NO2), so the true AQI could be higher if other pollutants are elevated. Real-time AQI also uses EPA's NowCast (a weighted ~12-hour average) rather than instantaneous readings.
